## Step 2: benchmarks. Al done; Pb, Nb, MgB2 and H3S configured, not run

### Software findings

- **QE 7.6 bug.** conda-forge QE 7.6 `ph.x` segfaults for electron–phonon with ANY ultrasoft or PAW pseudopotential. The cause is a source bug: `elphon.f90` calls `adddvscf` with 2 of its 3 arguments. The fix is reported to be upstream QEF/q-e!2963 (not re-verified from here because of GitLab rate limiting). QE 7.4 and 7.5 work. See `qe_eph_uspaw_segfault_repro.md` and the draft feedstock issue `qe_feedstock_issue_DRAFT.md` (NOT posted).
- **Pseudopotentials.** All benchmarks therefore use norm-conserving SG15 ONCV 1.2. These are also what EPW needs. Choices and cutoffs are in `step2_pseudopotentials_and_configs.md`.
- **QE version sensitivity.** The bundled QE 6.7 smoke fixture is version-specific. On that deliberately unconverged 8³ grid, QE 7.6 gives λ up to about 11% different at the larger broadenings. QE's own `lambda` file shows the same difference, so it is not a parsing issue.

### Al (fcc, 0 GPa): REAL DFPT

Setup: QE 7.6, PBE, SG15, 60/240 Ry, vc-relaxed (a = 4.022 Å), k 16³ / fit 32³, q 8³ (29 irreducible q), 10 smearings of 0.005–0.05 Ry. Wall time 55 min on 8 ranks.

**`rtsc run` verdict: ERROR, the correct conservative behaviour.**
- Smearing σ₁ = 0.005 Ry has 28 slightly negative α²F bins (−0.04% of A), and the default `negative_policy="error"` refuses them.
- Clipping is also refused: it shifts σ₁'s λ by 0.36%, which fails the footer consistency check.
- σ₁ is below what a 32³ grid resolves. Its λ of 0.428 is the outlier, while σ₂–σ₁₀ agree to 0.9%.

**Analysis with σ₁ excluded explicitly.** This was done with `bench_tools/analyze_benchmark.py`, outside the pipeline. **This is a choice the user still has to approve before it becomes a pipeline verdict.**

| quantity | rtsc (σ₂–σ₁₀) | literature (quoted from memory, unverified) |
|---|---|---|
| λ | 0.411–0.415 | 0.38–0.44 |
| ω_log | 304–308 K | ~270–320 K |
| A = ∫α²F | 67.1–67.5 K | – |

**Proved results** (rtsc.certify.classify, every verdict rechecked; statements about Tc_model):

| T* | μ*_hi | status | certified bracket [T_low, T_up] |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 K | 0.15 | REJECTED_PROVEN (R-UNIV) | T_up = 17.1 K |
| 1.2 K | 0.10 | CERTIFIED_CANDIDATE | [1.297, 10.30] K |
| 1.2 K | 0.15 | UNDECIDED | [0.578, 10.30] K |

Experimental Tc = 1.175 K.

**Reading the result:**
- Experiment lies inside the μ* ≤ 0.15 bracket, and every float Tc lies inside its proved bracket. **No consistency violation.**
- At μ*_hi = 0.10 the certified lower bound (1.30 K) is *above* experiment. That is not a contradiction. It shows that μ* in rtsc's convention (block cutoff at ω_c = 10·ω_max ≈ 4700 K) is not the literature's μ*. In this convention Al needs μ* above 0.10 to match experiment.
- The float Eliashberg reference (not proof) gives Tc = 1.15–1.20 K at μ* = 0.15, on top of the experimental value. Allen–Dynes is not comparable at weak coupling because it uses a different μ* convention.
- T_up ≈ 10 K is about 9× experiment. That is by design: it must hold for every μ* ≥ 0.

Data: `data/Al_benchmark/`, including the verdict JSONs and `rtsc recheck` commands.

## Step 3: first real MatterGen run + MatterSim prescreen. Done

- **MatterGen** (own Python 3.10 venv, torch 2.2.1+cu121, RTX 4090) generated 16 Mg-B structures in about 4 min with the `chemical_system_energy_above_hull` checkpoint. A second run of 16 went through rtsc's adapter end to end.
- **Adapter:** it parses the real `generated_crystals.extxyz` and the CIF-zip fallback identically. Provenance is complete and HYPOTHETICAL_STRUCTURE is set. MatterGen regenerated real MgB2 (P6/mmm).

  - **H3S Im-3m: the 5M model calls it stable at 150 and 200 GPa.** The 1M model's instability (−451 cm⁻¹ at H) reproduces, so the step-4 concern is specific to the 1M model.

    - crude boron fallback reference (1.5 eV/atom too high);
    - the hull cache never invalidates.
